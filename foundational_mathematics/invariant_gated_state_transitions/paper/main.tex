\begin{abstract}
We develop a theorem chain for guarded state systems in which a candidate update may be committed only after independently specified admissibility conditions close. The finite core begins from a structured initial datum with no accepted claims, introduces an invariant-preserving transition law, separates candidates from their validators, and proves exact conditions under which an observation operator determines a declared target. A quotient detects target-changing directions invisible to the observation, a canonical completion removes precisely those directions, and a positive-semidefinite inequality characterizes bounded target support. We next define an operational curvature by comparing two admissible local steps in opposite orders and prove that the resulting residue is exactly their commutator action. A finite proof-admission theorem is formulated on acyclic derivation graphs, followed by an all-obligation threshold theorem, a perturbation margin, a clock-independent fixed-state selector, a disjoint commit/hold/reject classification, exact refinement, and replay-complete append-only persistence. We then extend the target-support layer to arbitrary Hilbert spaces: a finite-channel adverse perturbation is governed exactly by a finite Hermitian compression, the minimum number of repair channels equals the target-relevant blind dimension, strict spectral reserve gives a quantitative source-relative positivity margin, and orthogonal source completion yields a positive projection defect. Thus finite certification is tied to finite target-relevant obstruction rank rather than to finite ambient dimension.
\end{abstract}
